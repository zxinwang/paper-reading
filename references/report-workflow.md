# Full paper reading and interactive report

Read this reference for a full report. The 13-part research flow and evidence rules in `SKILL.md` remain binding. Do not let a short deadline, a summary-shaped example, a fixed word count, or the presence of headings substitute for substantive paper-specific analysis. Adapt to paper type and reader language; sections that do not apply should explain why.

## Research before presentation

1. Read the entire available paper, relevant appendix and supplement; inspect tables, figures and equations visually as needed. Record exact pages, equation/table/figure numbers and what was unavailable. Read official code/data or inspect documentation when appropriate, and distinguish actual execution from inspection.
2. Build a claim-to-evidence map: question and motivation; each contribution and its mechanism; inputs, outputs, assumptions and failure points; key equations and definitions; experiment/baseline/metric/sample size/conditions, measured result and what remains unproven. Search and verify representative prior and later work, including alternatives and criticism. Keep preprint versions and published versions distinct.
3. Identify venue and paper status from publisher/DOI or official conference proceedings. For a journal, find the latest verifiable Journal Impact Factor (JIF) and JCR category quartile separately, giving metric year, subject category, source and access date. If requested or reliably accessible, report the Chinese Academy of Sciences partition separately with its year, edition and source; never equate it with JCR Q1–Q4 or SJR quartile. A journal may have several JCR categories and quartiles. If authoritative current data is inaccessible, show “not verified” rather than guessing or substituting a third-party score. A preprint has no journal JIF or journal quartile unless a published journal version is independently verified; a conference is not assigned a journal JIF. A journal metric is not evidence for an individual article's quality.
4. Plan an actual tutorial, not 13 one-paragraph summaries: explain the real motivating example and background before details, then connect intuition to modules and equations, and experiments to the exact claims. For central modules describe inputs, transformation, outputs, rationale, assumptions, and what happens when they fail. Derive important equations in readable steps with defined symbols and assumptions; explain the chain of reasoning and a concrete worked example. For experiments reconstruct design, comparison fairness, numerical denominator, absolute versus relative improvements, limitations and alternative explanations. If the evidence or mathematics is insufficient, say so explicitly.
5. Make the final four phases substantive: a resource-aware seven-day plan with actions, outputs, metrics and success criteria; at least relevant controlled falsification scenarios; documented follow-up search scope and verified primary links; and a testable candidate idea with baselines and risks, not an unsupported novelty claim.

## Tutorial layout and interactions

Start from the user's accepted reference style when provided, without reusing that paper's claims or copyrighted figures as if they belonged to another paper. The bundled shell uses a dark teal hero, mint accents, a desktop left sidebar, restrained section cards, and a collapsible mobile contents menu. Preserve this coherent design when the user requests that style; do not substitute a horizontal row of numbered, default-underlined links. Always display the complete original paper title prominently, even when the hero also has a reader-friendly slogan. Keep bibliographic details directly visible. Use generous line length and spacing, varied explanatory cards, visually distinct evidence tags, meaningful tables, and responsive layouts. A well-designed long report is preferred to a dense wall of text or a uniform series of identical boxes. Show at-a-glance findings, a concise reading route, and then the full 13-part explanation, not just a dashboard.

For every important concept, explain in the prose on first use. Supply a beginner glossary that remains easy to open while scrolling. The default is a floating panel that can actually be dragged by its title bar, with controls for docking to the side, returning to a float, resetting and closing. A lower-right launcher is one option, not a fixed requirement. Reuse the embedded behavior in the shell or read `assets/glossary-interactions.js` and `assets/glossary-panel.css`; embed the required code in the delivered HTML so it remains self-contained. Its panel is searchable, scrollable and keyboard-operable, with plain definition, a concrete example and the term's role in this paper. Clickable in-text terms should open the corresponding entry; keep a non-JavaScript glossary fallback. Clamp floating coordinates to the viewport, including after resizing or reopening. Support touch-pointer movement, arrow-key movement and resetting. On narrow screens, avoid hiding content or covering essential controls.

Create several interactions driven by the paper's actual content, not decorative controls: an interactive pipeline with stage detail; an evidence-linked experiment comparison with visible data and denominators; expandable derivation steps; and, when defensible, a parameter-controlled teaching model or scenario simulator. Label models as illustrative and define their assumptions and inputs. Add problem-specific SVG diagrams, data-flow visuals, diagrams of assumptions and failures, and charts tied to source values. For claims, enable a reader to locate the underlying paper section, figure or table. Different papers may call for different interactions; never invent science to fill a feature quota.

Deliver a real, mostly self-contained file with embedded content, CSS, JavaScript and SVG; use offline-capable formula rendering, not raw unrendered LaTeX. Avoid unnecessary external fonts/libraries; declare unavoidable online dependencies. Distinguish redrawn diagrams from original figures and follow source licensing. Maintain clear contrast, focus indicators, labels, keyboard access and reduced-motion behavior. The shell is an aid, not a replacement for writing paper-specific content or checking accuracy.

## Responsive readability is a requirement

Give every hero paragraph, author line, source link and emphasized word a legible foreground on the dark background. Reuse the shell's inherited light hero text; do not rely on a default body paragraph color. Check normal text contrast against a 4.5:1 target, including metadata.

Do not shrink a desktop-wide SVG until its labels become miniature on a phone. At the narrow target width, keep rendered diagram labels at least about 12 CSS pixels. Reflow into a vertical/mobile diagram or use the bundled `.diagram-scroll` wrapper with a readable minimum SVG width, an obvious horizontal-scroll hint and equivalent prose. A desktop viewBox 1,000 units wide with 20-unit text becomes unreadable when squeezed into 300 pixels. Check the actual scaled text, not just the SVG's declared font-size. Apply the same principle to chart labels and equations.

Validate touch controls after dragging, not only initial opening: touch-drag the title, then tap reset and assert the panel really returns to its default viewport position; tap dock, float and close and assert state and geometry. A click handler being present or a tap being dispatched does not establish success. Reuse the shell's current embedded runtime rather than reimplementing only part of the behavior.

## Acceptance before delivery

- Check all 13 applicable sections for explanatory substance and source links, including assumptions, uncertainty, data provenance and version. Explicitly identify any inaccessible source or missing verification.
- Recalculate key figures and units from original tables; verify formulas against the paper, source markers and any journal metric's actual year and category. Never claim independent replication when none was run.
- Open the saved file in a real browser. Test every interaction by changing its state and checking visible results: glossary launcher/open/search/term link/close across scroll, actual pointer dragging with a measured change in panel position, dock/float/reset, keyboard movement, viewport resize and reopening, every pipeline stage, comparison controls, derivation expansion, desktop and mobile navigation and any slider. Record browser errors, console errors, broken images, broken formulas and missing local dependencies. Test with network blocked where offline is claimed.
- Inspect actual screenshots at desktop and mobile widths at top, method, mathematics, experiments and glossary. Check overlapping fixed controls, clipping, legible formula and table overflow, keyboard focus and content accessibility. Repair and rerun failing checks. A DOM-only or no-overflow test is not visual review.
- Report the real scope of the check, unresolved limitations and the output path. If resources prevent the requested depth or validation, do not describe the output as complete; provide the most useful verified work and disclose the remaining work.
